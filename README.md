Coastal Union Bank Business Continuity, Disaster Recovery & Crisis Management
This project develops a Crisis Communication Plan and Tabletop Exercise for Coastal Union Bank, focusing on responding to a major core banking system outage. It outlines the crisis activation process, Crisis Management Team responsibilities, customer communication approach, and recovery coordination required to maintain essential banking operations during a disruption.

Project Overview
The purpose of this project is to assess Coastal Union Bank’s preparedness to manage a major disruption affecting its core banking system, payment processing, branches, and digital banking services.

The project focuses on three key areas:
- Crisis Communication Plan: Establishing a structured approach to communicating with customers and stakeholders during service disruptions.
- Crisis Activation Path: Defining the authority and procedures for declaring a crisis and activating the Crisis Management Team (CMT).
- Tabletop Exercise: Evaluating the bank’s ability to coordinate incident response, prioritize critical banking services, maintain business continuity, and manage reputational risks during a prolonged system outage.
The exercise uses a simulated core banking system outage to examine the bank’s response to operational disruption, delayed payments, increasing customer complaints, and public attention.

Configuration Steps
1. Develop the Crisis Communication Plan
- Prepare a customer notification explaining the temporary disruption to banking services.
- Identify the affected services, including transactions and online banking.
- Communicate that the IT team is working to restore services.
- Provide an expected time for the next update through official communication channels.
- Direct customers to the customer support team for assistance.
2. Establish the Crisis Activation Path
- Assign the Chief Operating Officer (COO) responsibility for declaring a crisis based on the Incident Commander’s assessment of the outage and its operational impact.
- Designate the Chief Executive Officer (CEO) as the alternate authority if the COO is unavailable.
- Require the Incident Commander to notify the COO and recommend activating the Crisis Management Team.
- Obtain COO authorization before activating the CMT.
- Contact team members using the emergency contact list, phone calls, or approved emergency communication channels.
- Convene the CMT immediately, virtually or in person, to coordinate recovery priorities, business continuity, and communication.
3. Assign Crisis Management Team Responsibilities
- Incident Commander: Leads and coordinates the crisis response.
- IT Recovery Lead: Investigates the outage and restores banking systems.
- Operations Lead: Coordinates business continuity arrangements.
- Risk and Legal Lead: Advises on regulatory and legal obligations.
- Communications Lead: Manages approved customer, employee, and public updates.
- Business Recovery Leads: Coordinate affected departments and report operational impacts.
4. Conduct the Tabletop Exercise – Core Banking System Outage
- Establish the initial scenario at 8:30 a.m. on Monday, when the IT team discovers that the core banking system hosted at the primary data centre is unavailable.
- Simulate the impact on branches, customer account access, transaction processing, mobile banking, and online banking.
- Require the Incident Commander to inform the COO and recommend declaring a crisis and activating the CMT.
- Assess how the bank would respond when the cause and estimated recovery time are initially unknown.
5. Introduce Escalation Inject 1 – Payment Processing Disruption
- Simulate a 10:00 a.m. escalation in which the IT team confirms that payment processing is also affected.
- Introduce delayed customer transactions and approaching payment and settlement deadlines.
- Discuss how the CMT should prioritize critical banking services.
- Identify alternative arrangements for urgent transactions.
- Determine how affected customers and payment partners should be informed.
6. Introduce Escalation Inject 2 – Customer and Reputational Impact
- Simulate a 12:00 p.m. escalation in which the outage remains unresolved.
- Introduce increasing customer complaints on social media and an enquiry from a local news outlet.
- Assess how the bank should respond to public concerns without speculating about the cause of the outage.
- Determine who approves public communications.
- Discuss how customer support can manage increased demand and how frequently the CMT should provide updates.
7. Evaluate Response and Recovery Preparedness
- Assess whether the CMT can activate promptly and assign clear responsibilities.
- Review how critical banking services would be prioritized and restored.
- Evaluate communication with customers and relevant stakeholders.
- Identify operational and reputational risks arising from the outage.
- Document weaknesses and corrective actions to improve the Business Continuity and Disaster Recovery plans.
Results and Findings
The tabletop exercise provides a structured way to evaluate Coastal Union Bank’s preparedness for a major core banking system outage.

Expected outcomes include:
- Crisis Activation: The bank should be able to activate the Crisis Management Team promptly, with clear authority and responsibilities.
- Operational Continuity: The exercise should assess the bank’s ability to prioritize critical banking services and coordinate recovery activities.
- Payment Recovery: The bank should identify alternative arrangements for urgent transactions when payment processing is disrupted.
- Crisis Communication: The bank should maintain timely, accurate, and approved communication with customers, employees, payment partners, and the public.
- Reputational Risk Management: The bank should be able to address customer concerns and media enquiries without making unconfirmed statements about the incident.
- Customer Support: The exercise should assess how customer support can manage increased enquiries and complaints during prolonged service interruptions.
- Business Continuity and Disaster Recovery Improvement: Identified weaknesses should be documented, with corrective actions used to strengthen the bank’s continuity and recovery plans.
The scenario highlights the importance of clear crisis declaration authority, defined team responsibilities, coordinated IT and business recovery activities, and consistent customer communication. It also demonstrates the need to prepare for escalating operational and reputational impacts when a system outage continues for several hours.

The expected findings will help Coastal Union Bank identify gaps in its crisis management arrangements and improve its Business Continuity and Disaster Recovery plans.
